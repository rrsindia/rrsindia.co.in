Local or cloud? The honest version

A desktop system in your own office is not a bad choice, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something. It does several things genuinely well. It also costs you in ways that stay invisible until the day they do not. Here is the whole picture, both sides.

See it on your own books Ask us anything
Starting fairly

What a desktop system genuinely does well

These are real advantages. They are the reason so many good businesses still run this way, and they deserve to be said plainly before anything else.

🔌

It works with no internet

The line goes down and billing carries on. In an area with unreliable connectivity that is not a small thing, it is the whole argument.

🏢

The data sits in your building

You can point at the machine. For a lot of owners that is worth more than any feature list, and it is a perfectly reasonable way to feel.

⚡

It is fast on a local network

No round trip to anywhere. A big report on a well set up office network is quick.

🤝

Nobody can switch it off

No vendor decides your access. If the company you bought it from closes tomorrow, the software on your machine keeps running.

The other side of the same coin

Where it quietly costs you

None of these show up on any invoice. Most owners meet them once, and remember it.

💾

It is one hard disk

Drives fail. When that one fails, the books of the business are on it. Everything below is a variation of this single sentence.

🗑

Backups depend on somebody remembering

Almost every business we have seen intended to back up weekly. Ask when the last one actually ran, and then ask whether anyone has ever opened it to check it works.

🚩

A backup in the same room is not a backup

The external drive in the same drawer does not survive a fire, a flood, or the person who takes the computer.

🚗

You have to be at that desk

A question at 9pm, a figure needed while you are travelling, a query from a buyer on a Sunday. All of it waits until you are back in front of the machine.

📤

Your CA works from a copy

You send a backup file. From that moment there are two versions of your books, yours and theirs, and they start drifting apart the same afternoon.

🔧

One person knows how it is set up

Where the data lives, which version, what the licence key is, how it was configured. When they are unavailable, so is the answer.

What actually changes

On cloud, the machine stops mattering

Not a longer feature list. A different relationship between your books and the computer they used to live on.

🌎

Your books are not on your computer

The laptop is stolen, the desktop dies, the office floods. You log in from anything else and carry on. There is nothing on that machine to lose.

🔄

Backups happen whether anyone remembers or not

Every night, automatically, to storage in a different place from the server. Nobody has to do anything, and nobody can forget.

📱

The office is wherever you are

Any browser, and a real Android app for staff who work on the move. The same books, not a summary or a report someone emailed you.

👥

Several people, at the same time

Billing counter, accountant, owner, all in the books at once. No merging, no waiting, no asking who has the file open.

📈

Your CA sees the same books you do

No file to send. Nothing to reconcile between two copies. They open the live books, with your permission, whenever they need to.

✨

Improvements just arrive

No installer, no disruption, no version where half the staff are on last year’s. What we build reaches you without you doing anything.

The fair objections

What cloud asks of you

Four real concerns. We would rather answer them here than have you discover them later.

📶

“It needs internet”

True, and it is the honest trade. In practice a basic broadband line or a phone hotspot is enough, and the Android app is built for a weak connection rather than a perfect one.

Worth knowing: if your connection is genuinely unreliable for hours at a time, say so before you buy and we will tell you straight whether this suits you.
🏦

“My data is on someone else’s server”

It is, and you should know exactly whose and where. Your books sit on Amazon Web Services infrastructure in Mumbai. Your data stays in India.

And inside the software: every change is recorded with who made it, when, and the value before and after. That applies to us as much as to your own staff.
❓

“What if you stop existing”

The right question, and most vendors dodge it. Your data is backed up every night to separate off-site storage, and we have restored from those backups end to end to prove they work.

The difference: we test the restore rather than assume it. A backup nobody has ever restored is not a backup, it is a hope.
💵

“It is a yearly cost”

Also true. Set it against what it replaces: no server to buy, no backup drive, no reinstall visit when a machine dies, no paid upgrade every few years, no data recovery bill.

Be sceptical of “one time”: desktop software rarely is. Support, upgrades and the hardware under it all recur, just less visibly.

For the accountant

What changes for your CA

This is where the difference stops being about hardware and starts being about how the two of you work together.

1
🔒

You give access, and you can take it back

Your CA sees your books because you granted it. You choose the level of access and which years it covers, and you can withdraw it at any time. Nothing is shared without your permission.

2
👀

They work on live books, not last month’s copy

No collecting files before a deadline, no discovering that the version they worked on is three weeks behind yours. One set of books, always current.

📋

Who entered it, when, and what it looked like before. When a figure moves, neither of you has to reconstruct why from memory.

Straight answers

Questions we actually get asked

Is my data safer on my own computer than on your server? +
It depends entirely on what you mean by safer. Against someone reaching it over the internet, a machine with no internet connection is genuinely harder to reach. Against the far more common ways businesses actually lose their books, a failed drive, a theft, a fire, or a backup nobody ever tested, the office machine is the weaker of the two. Most losses we have seen were nothing to do with an attacker.
Can your staff see or change my books? +
We can reach the system to support and maintain it, which is true of anyone hosting anything, including whoever maintains your office computer. The difference is that inside the software every change is recorded with who made it and what it looked like before, and that record applies to us exactly as it applies to your own staff. We do not make entries in a client’s books.
What happens if my internet is down for a day? +
You would not be able to bill from the office line, and a phone hotspot is the usual answer, as most billing is a small amount of data. We would rather say that plainly than pretend it is not a real limitation. If your connection is genuinely out for hours at a stretch, tell us before you buy and we will give you an honest view on whether this suits your situation.
Can I get my own data out? +
Yes, and you should ask this of every vendor before you buy, not after. Your data is backed up nightly to off-site storage, and we have restored from those backups to confirm they actually work rather than assuming they do. Ask us to show you.
Do I have to move everything at once? +
No. Most businesses start at the beginning of a financial year with opening balances and masters brought across, and keep the old system available for reference for as long as they want it. Nobody has to switch off anything on day one.
Is a browser app slower than software on my machine? +
On a large report over a slow line, a local system can feel quicker, and we will not claim otherwise. For everyday billing and entry the difference is not something you notice. The best way to settle it is to try it on your own connection with your own data, which is what the trial is for.

The only way to actually decide

Read comparisons for as long as you like, then spend twenty minutes in the thing itself with your own figures. We will set it up for you, and there is nothing to install.
