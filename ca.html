The part most software skips

An audit trail that stands up

Rule 3(1) of the Companies (Accounts) Rules requires books kept in software that records an edit log of every change and cannot be switched off. Rule 11(g) then asks you to report on it. Here is how we answer.

🔎

Every change, before and after

Who made it, when, from which address, and the value before and after. Open any entry and read the change field by field.

🔒

It cannot be switched off

There is no setting, no flag and no screen that disables it. No route in the application can edit or delete an entry, and entries are kept for eight years.

🛡️

Tampering is detectable

Each entry is sealed with a fingerprint of its own contents and the entry before it. Press Verify integrity and the software re-checks every entry and names the exact one that broke, if any.

📆

Scoped to your engagement

As the engaged auditor you see the period your engagement covers. The screen says so plainly rather than quietly narrowing the view.

Every client in one place with turnover, invoice counts and last activity. One click to open a client, one click to come back.

GST that ties to the books

GSTR-1 with HSN and document summary, GSTR-3B, and 2A/2B reconciliation. The outward value reconciles to the P&L, net of credit notes, because both are built from the same entries.

Questions CAs ask

Can I use this for a company client that I audit? +
Yes. The audit trail records every change with its before and after values, cannot be disabled, is preserved for eight years, and can be verified for tampering from inside the software. That is what Rule 3(1) asks of the books, and what Rule 11(g) asks you to report on.
What if my other clients use different software? +
The dashboard shows the clients whose books are on RRFinEApp. Clients on other software stay where they are. Most practices move one or two clients first and add more as they see it working.
Do my clients need to install anything? +
No. It runs in the browser, and there is an Android app for staff who work on the move.
Who can post entries, and who can close the year? +
Whoever you decide. On a client you audit you hold auditor rights: you see everything and can export it, and posting and year-end stay with the client. On a client whose books you keep, you are the administrator and run the year-end yourself.
Is my clients' data separate? +
Yes. Each client's books are kept separate from every other customer, on AWS-managed PostgreSQL with nightly backups.
Is it really an alternative to Tally or Busy? +
It keeps the Dr/Cr discipline and keyboard-first speed your staff already know, and adds multi-company access, a verifiable audit trail and cloud access from anywhere. See RRFinEApp for the full comparison.
